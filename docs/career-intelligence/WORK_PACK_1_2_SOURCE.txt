5. Track C | Revised shared identity instructions 

Instruction design reference: CI-IC-REV-12-DRAFT. Proposed consolidated implementation wording for this revision; not the verbatim CI-RIC-02 test bundle. Version and review it as a new bundle. Store shared policy outside provider adapters. Use the following identity block in discovery and any authorised identity reconciliation. 

A capability is an underlying ability that can reasonably be assessed and recorded independently. Confine discovery to the occupational territory represented by the supplied source; do not target a capability count or generate an exhaustive occupational ontology. 

For every material expression, define the substantive action or judgement and its object or purpose. Identify constituent abilities before the counterfactual: could a credible person sufficiently demonstrate one constituent while legitimately having weaker or no evidence for another? If yes, presume separate identities, subject to subsumption. 

Subsumption is justified only where separation would distinguish methods, techniques, tools, technologies, procedural steps, contexts or manifestations of the same underlying ability. Name that shared ability. Common workflow, occupation, outcome or performer does not settle identity. Applying a method and interpreting information may require separate abilities. 

Setting, seniority, supervision, autonomy, delegated authority, responsibility, complexity and scale are attributes unless they materially change the underlying ability. Routine or informal activity is not automatically a separate identity, and low complexity does not disqualify an independently assessable ability. 

Assist, support and participate do not by themselves define an ability. Do not invent the missing personal action. A defensible identity may exist while a source association or the candidate’s contribution remains unclear. Keep occupational identity, source meaning, personal contribution and historical-role attribution as distinct decisions. 

If source meaning is unsettled, preserve the exact wording/location, competing meanings and possible associations as an unresolved source signal. If the occupational boundary is unsettled, preserve alternatives as an identity governance item. Missing candidate detail alone is not an occupational governance problem. Keep both records where both issues exist. 

Record decisions as accept provisional identity, split, subsume under a named ability, or refer for governance review. Include constituent comparison, subsumption result, references, preserved attributes and a concise justification. Correct inconsistencies or retain uncertainty; do not force a decision. Qualification, title, certification or repository correspondence does not prove personal application or canonical approval. 

Operation boundary 

Discovery returns provisional identities, decisions, unresolved signals, governance items and source coverage. It does not grade evidence, generate questions or approve knowledge. The application reviews/freezes the intended register before independent evidence assessment. A changed meaning creates an explicit new revision/supersession record; an identifier must not silently acquire a different meaning.

7. Track C | Enrichment, answers, edits and review 

Materiality and question planning 

Prioritise clarification only where the missing information could materially change capability meaning, evidence sufficiency for a relevant ability or profile usefulness. Record investigate_now or defer, its reason and target references. Deferral does not establish E3, inapplicability, resolved mapping or a competence gap. Do not assume a target vacancy. 

Ask one focused, neutral evidence-bearing question for a specific unresolved contribution or essential missing element. Do not ask for a measurable result or every procedural step by default. Do not introduce evidence, lead the candidate to claim an action or repeatedly ask for an ability the evidence already establishes. Occupational definition questions belong to governance when candidate recall cannot settle them. 

Answer evaluation 

Validate the originating question, role, target identity and definition revision. Evaluate each supplied question independently. A shared episode may support multiple abilities only through independent assessments. Sufficient evidence resolves the originating question without an artificial follow-up; insufficient evidence gets a meaningful question and reason only where further clarification is useful and material. 

Always scan the answer for additional independent capability signals, even when the originating question is resolved. Reconcile identities and duplicates before writes. Do not stage a new signal as demonstrated without an independent sufficient assessment. If an existing identity already exists, retain the new source contribution rather than silently dropping it. 

Edits and profile approval 

Treat candidate edits as new intelligence. Reassess the edited capability/evidence against the exact definition revision; do not inherit the previous outcome. Preserve earlier evidence and evaluations as history. If meaning changes, return an explicit identity-change proposal and review dependency rather than silently renaming a canonical identity. 

Use first-person wording for candidate-owned evidence, including answer and emerging-capability paths. Preserve exact source excerpts separately. Candidate approval confirms the candidate’s account; it does not independently verify competence or automatically approve an occupational identity. 

Completion and holds 

Define the product’s completion eligibility using evidence disposition, unresolved issues and review authorisation separately. Until an explicit reviewed rule permits otherwise, unresolved material row holds or missing required decisions block promotion; return a reviewable explanation. Do not convert holds to gaps to pass a gate. Current snapshots do not establish this full eligibility policy; include the signed-off rule in the implementation payload. 

Invalid model output, refusal, timeout and unavailable dependencies are processing outcomes, not evidence grades. Retain user input and historical state. No provider error may downgrade a candidate, settle a hold or manufacture a completed role.

12. Track D | Processing-state UX standard 

Standing rule CI-UX-001: Every user-triggered processing action must immediately acknowledge the action, prevent duplicate/conflicting actions, communicate its processing state, and provide a clear success or recoverable failure outcome without losing user input. Apply this to existing in-scope screens and every future code implementation. 

D-01 | Shared state ownership 

Implement one reusable action-processing mechanism and shared busy-button pattern. On invocation, set the active screen’s operation state synchronously before awaiting work; lock its action buttons and conflicting navigation. Do not rely only on a spinner or a delayed render to stop double clicks. Cover keyboard submission and programmatic form submission as well. 

The server must independently protect mutations against duplicate/replayed requests. State is scoped to the operation and affected screen; do not attempt to disable browser controls or unrelated screens. Preserve input and submitted revision. Any supported cancellation must be a distinct safe control, not an unguarded second mutation. 

State

Processing

Confirmed result, known failure or uncertain timeout.

Retry is safe after known failure/non-commit. 

D-02 | End-state discipline 

Use a single owner for setting and clearing busy state; ensure every success/error branch reaches a defined outcome. A try/finally pattern may release local resources, but must not blindly unlock a conflicting mutation when server completion is unknown. Do not leave controls disabled indefinitely: provide bounded reconciliation, a visible problem state and a recovery route. 

D-03 | Completion confirmation 

For Complete role, keep processing visible through validation, persistence and completion result. Show success only when the server confirms the committed outcome. Read back authoritative lifecycle/profile state as required; a cleared spinner or optimistic UI update is not proof of completion.

Before profile approval 

Your profile is a draft based on the information you provide. Please check that each statement accurately describes what you personally did, including work you shared or completed under supervision. Describe what you personally did and, where relevant, the tools or techniques you used. If you worked with others, make your own contribution clear. Shared or supervised work is equally useful—just explain your part. 

A short, specific example helps us build a fuller and more accurate profile. You do not need to describe every step or provide a measurable result. If you only observed an activity, did not perform part of it, or cannot recall an example, please say so.
